7 · The Weight of Small Things

Most of us imagine discipline as a single dramatic act: the alarm at five, the cold shower, the vow made on the first of January. But the people I interviewed described something far quieter. They spoke of discipline as a kind of equanimity, a steadiness that let them return to the work on the days it felt pointless.

A retired bridge engineer in Pune told me he had written one page every morning for thirty-one years. His pagework, he called it. “Some pages were terrible,” he said. “That was never the point. The point was that the page existed.”

What struck me was how little he relied on motivation. Motivation, in his telling, was weather: pleasant when it arrived, useless to plan around. The habit was the house he had built so the weather wouldn’t matter.

This is the weight of small things. A single page is nothing. Three hundred pages is a book.
